Up Rock history: where the dance began

Up Rock was born on the streets of Bushwick, Brooklyn, in the late 1960s. The Dynasty Rockers' own accounts place its birth there in 1968. This timeline follows the main line of the story, from the first street corners to the rockers of Russia, Poland and Japan.

The people who were there do not always agree on dates. Where they differ, most entries give more than one.

The roots: late 1960s

Before there were dance crews, the dance lived with the street gangs.

  1. Late 1960s Gangs burn to music

    Up Rock was inspired by the street fights between gangs. At first rockers imitated those fights with the gestures of the street; as the dance was practiced, they added the interpretation of the music and gestural jokes, mocking the other or playing with the instruments and the lyrics of the record, until it became a dance style of its own. In many cases, gang fights turned into Apache Lines: dance battles in place of violence. By one veteran’s account, gangs in other places learned it from their own chapters (local branches) in Brooklyn.

    When he was young there were gangs on the street, and the dance pulled him away from them. A passion, practiced with discipline, can open another path.

  2. 1967–1968 Born in Bushwick

    Up Rock was born in Bushwick, Brooklyn, in the late 1960s; the Dynasty Rockers’ own accounts give 1968. Two dancers created it, Rubberband Man and Apache, playing and having fun in the street: Rubberband, who also danced and won contests in other dances, and Apache, the joker. Rubberband Man is no longer with us.

    From Brooklyn the dance crossed the Williamsburg Bridge, which joins Brooklyn and Manhattan, and reached the streets and parks of the Lower East Side, as a rocker from that neighborhood writes.

  3. 1968–1970 A first generation

    By one veteran’s account, a first generation of rockers was active from 1968–69 or 1969–70, among them Little Dave, whose Little Dave Rockers grew into one of the big crews of the next decade. The same account says Brooklyn already had well-known DJs, such as Grandmaster Flowers.

The 1970s: crews, contests and the jerk

In the 1970s the dance got its crews, its contests and its signature move, the jerk. Crews battled on Bushwick’s streets and in its church halls. Dates in this decade are the most disputed, so most entries give more than one.

  1. 1971–1973 Learning on the corner

    Members of local gangs such as the Devil’s Rebels rocked on Bushwick street corners, and younger kids picked the dance up there.

  2. 1973 A contest in a church

    Older rockers remember the first rock contest in 1973 at St. Barbara’s Church in Bushwick. Crazy Rob, a Bushwick promoter, took rock contests from the streets into church halls and rec centers, as the Dynasty Rockers’ website tells it. Rockers of other crews remember his contests too, and in one veteran’s words, “the rock grew because of him”.

  3. 1973–1974 Dynasty and Touch of Rock

    The Dynasty Rockers date themselves to 1973. Their website names Danny Boy and Carlos as founders, with Manny and Eddie Figueroa among the first members, and by one member’s account Carlos took the name from a karate movie. Their great rival was Touch of Rock, with Buz, Cuz and Duz. It grew out of a group that Buz calls the Nightbreakers, and Duz dates the new name to October 1974; in Buz’s words, “We wanted to give it a little touch of rock”.

  4. 1973–1977 Names on the back

    Crews began wearing their name and the word “Rockers” on their shirts, although colors were a gang matter and could bring trouble. Depending on the account, Dynasty’s first lettering dates from 1973 or from 1976.

  5. 1974–1976 The jerk appears

    The jerk is a sudden movement of the body, usually down toward the floor, on an accent of the music, counted “1, 2, 3 and 4”. The rockers who were there do not agree on when it appeared. One Touch of Rock member says he saw it, and began learning it, in October 1974, while another, of the same crew, saw no jerking in 1975 or 1976, and a third rocker wrote that before 1976 there were no jerks.

  6. 1976–1979 Crews multiply

    By one veteran’s account, crews boomed in 1976–77, with more than a hundred in Bushwick alone. The Dynasty Rockers’ website recalls Apache lines of more than 50 rockers at some contests, and another veteran remembers lines of up to 40 people a side.

    Over time the name “uprock” appeared beside “rock”, the name many veterans still use. One veteran has dated the change both to the late 1970s and to the early 1970s, and tied it to confusion with rock fans at jams; the book Underground Dance Masters (2012) ties it to the Bushwick step spreading to other boroughs.

  7. Late 1970s The top rockers

    By the account of a Dynasty member of those years, the rockers to beat were Danny Boy and Carlos of Dynasty, Duz and Cuz of Touch of Rock, Rocky Nelson of MTC and Little Dave. Diana led Dynasty’s girls, and the same rocker calls his battle against her the best he ever had. Disco Eddie ranked his Disco Rockers third among the top three crews, after Dynasty and Touch of Rock.

  8. 1979 Into breaking

    By one veteran’s account, breaking was danced at a roller rink in Queens in 1979, and Breaking in Style, a Williamsburg breaking crew, gives 1979 as its founding year, although members’ profiles on the same site say 1980. How the two dances differ is explained on the Is Up Rock Top Rock? page.

The 1980s: beyond New York, and the fade

By the early 1980s there were signs of the dance outside New York. By the middle of the decade breaking had taken over the contests, and rocking almost disappeared.

  1. 1980 King Uprock

    As Ralph Casanova and the Dynasty Rockers’ website tell it, in 1980 crews such as Touch of Rock, the Lil Dave Rockers, the Romantic Rockers, Rock with Class and Dynasty met in a rock contest at the Yellow Sunshine club on Bushwick Avenue, and Ralph, then known as RC, beat Little Dave in the final. From then on he called himself King Uprock.

  2. 1981 Contests and the Bronx

    Rock contests came in two kinds: one-on-one battles and routines with a partner. That year a flyer for a club in the Bronx advertised a rock dance contest. The book Underground Dance Masters (2012) says the uprock step started being done in the Bronx in 1981.

  3. Early 1980s Beyond New York

    By the account of a dancer who grew up in Hartford, Connecticut, a gang there, the Savage Nomads from New York, did the dance and called it outlawing, and there was talk of rock dance contests in Holyoke, Massachusetts, around 1982–84. In Florida in 1983, by the same account, people simply called it Brooklyn, a name Joseph Schloss also records.

    Outlaws: the rockers’ word for gang members.

  4. 1983–1985 The fade

    By one veteran’s account, after 1983–84 the contests were all breaking contests, and the generation of rockers of the 1970s came to an end around 1985; another veteran says 1983 was probably the last time he rocked. A 1984 breakdancing manual gave uprock a section of its own and noted that more and more dancers did it solo, without an opponent.

The 1990s: looking for the originals

In the 1990s Ralph “King Uprock” Casanova brought the dance back, at the head of a small group that, by Break Easy’s account, included Mr. Loose, Burn One and Charlie Uprock. Most of the original dancers had stopped about a decade before; he looked for them, brought them in as judges and began to teach again. Some rockers call it the rebirth.

  1. 1993–1994 The first Dollar Jams

    Dollar Jams, monthly jams with a one-dollar entry started by Ralph Casanova, began in 1993 or 1994 by his dates, with free classes alongside them. There were trophies for breaking, uprock and popping. By his account Mr. Loose of Born To Rock started them with him, and Gary “Crumb” of Dynasty helped judge. In 1994 Charlie Uprock also held a rock competition with DJ Kool, Break Easy recalls.

  2. 1995 A new generation

    In early 1995 Ralph Casanova drew Break Easy, a breaker of Breaking in Style who had learned to rock as a boy in Williamsburg, back to rocking. From then on Break Easy taught a new generation of b-boys, and later rocking too; he joined Dynasty in 2002.

  3. 1997–1999 Looking for the originals

    In 1997–98 Ralph Casanova went looking for the original Brooklyn rockers. With the blessing of Danny Boy, one of the crew’s founders, he took over the Dynasty Rockers as, in his words, “the new founder from the 90s”. The Dynasty Rockers’ website of 2002 says two b-boys, Numbers of the T.N.T. breaking crew and Burn One, had started a search for the original rockers in the mid-1990s. Numbers joined Dynasty in 1998 and later became its vice president; by Break Easy’s account, he flew King Uprock to Miami.

  4. 1999 Ridgewood

    In 1999 Ralph Casanova of the Dynasty Rockers set up a practice session with the Greater Ridgewood Youth Council in the gym of I.S. 93 in Ridgewood, Queens, where he coached uprock. By 2002 the Village Voice called it one of the largest b-boy practice spots on the East Coast. Up to 100 people aged 6 to 30 came, and original b-boys taught them the basics, which dancers call the foundation, and the history of the form.

The 2000s: Folk Feet and trips abroad

In the 2000s Up Rock got a place in a Brooklyn folk dance program and reached Europe.

  1. 2002 Las Vegas, Florida, Switzerland

    By 2002 the Dynasty Rockers listed members in Las Vegas, Florida and Switzerland.

  2. 2003–2009 Folk Feet

    The Brooklyn Arts Council’s Folk Feet project documented and presented Brooklyn’s traditional dances from 2003 to 2009, uprocking among them.

  3. 2003 Thirty years of Dynasty

    On May 3, 2003, the Dynasty Rockers turned 30. Apache came in his Devil’s Rebels colors, Manny Figueroa, whom the crew’s site calls an “Uprock Pioneer,” received an award for his achievements in uprock history, and Karyn “the Lish” of Breaking in Style won the uprock contest against Mr. Loose of Born To Rock.

  4. 2005–2007 France and Poland

    In 2005 Wary, a French rocker from Dijon, joined the Dynasty Rockers, and the Dynasty Rockers took uprock to Total Session 7 in France. According to a 2019 Polish article, a video from that event drew dancers in Poland to the dance around 2006. In 2007, the article says, Wary taught a two-day workshop in Ostrów Wielkopolski for about 50 dancers, and that year Suseł and Wawix founded Rockin With Flavor, which the article calls Poland’s first rocking crew.

  5. 2009 Mastermind returns

    In 2009 Mighty Mike and Ringo, with the blessing of original members, started a new generation of the Mastermind Rockers, the Williamsburg crew. The crew says it has since taken the dance to Japan, Taiwan, South Korea and Poland.

2010 to 2019: Russia, Poland and Asia

From about 2010 Russian dancers, FT among them, invited the Dynasty Rockers to Russia, and by the crew’s account the visits went on almost every year until the pandemic. Rockers from Dynasty and Mastermind visited Poland, and crews there and in Asia grew their own scenes.

  1. 2010–2012 Tours of Europe

    Cold Chris, a Las Vegas-based Dynasty Rocker, made three tours of Europe between 2010 and 2012, teaching in Zamość, Toruń and Żagań, and brought Foots of Dynasty on his 2012 Live To Rock tour. According to the 2019 Polish article, Poland also hosted Amigo of Dynasty in 2010, Ringo of Mastermind in 2011 and Break Easy in 2012.

  2. 2011 Thirty-eight years in Phoenix

    The Dynasty Rockers held their 38th anniversary in Phoenix, Arizona, on October 29, 2011.

  3. 2013 Tver

    Russian TV covered a rock contest in Tver for the Dynasty Rockers’ 40th anniversary, held by For The Rock, which the presenter called the only rock crew in the country at the time. Its member FT is, by Dynasty’s own account, the Dynasty Rockers’ vice president in Russia.

  4. 2018–2019 A line of 150

    In 2018 FT was at the Yalta Summer Jam, where Grinch won the rock dance contest. In 2019 Russian TV covered For The Rock’s tenth anniversary. A tenth-anniversary event in Russia, recalled in a 2022 interview as “like four years ago”, put 150 rockers in an Apache line.

  5. 2019 St. Petersburg

    The best rockers now live in Russia, with Poland, China and Korea also at a good level, according to an interview recorded at a festival in St. Petersburg.

Today

For more than two decades Ralph “King Uprock” Casanova has been the one carrying the dance, with the Dynasty Rockers and the Brooklyn Arts Council, in New York and abroad. Today the dance has crews on several continents. Ralph says Dynasty has members in Russia, Las Vegas, Boston, China, Korea, Spain (Moysex, a Dynasty Rocker since 2006) and Colombia.

  1. 2020 For The Record

    Brooklyn veterans met for “For The Record,” a round table published by Vitamin Vision TV, so the early generations could tell their story. They disagreed openly on dates. That year, in another Vitamin Vision TV interview, the interviewer asked why New York, and even the United States, could not gather 20 solid rockers for a contest, while lines of 15 to 50 formed in Russia, Korea or Japan. The answer: “It’s the negativity, it’s the lies, the disrespect.” In Russia, by contrast, “They believe in show and prove”.

  2. 2021–2022 A magazine in Japan

    From November 1, 2021, to November 21, 2022, a Japanese group published “Weekly UPROCK MAG” on note.com: 58 articles, almost all on uprock, among them interviews with the OG Frank Rojas and a translated interview with Ringo. It also followed today’s crews, from Ultimate Style Rockers in South Korea to Jackey Burn of Mastermind in Taiwan.
