Up Rock history: where the dance began

Up Rock was born on the streets of Bushwick, Brooklyn, in the late 1960s. Most accounts place its birth there around 1968. This timeline follows the main line of the story, from the first street corners to the rockers of Russia, Poland and Japan.

The people who were there do not always agree on dates. Where they differ, the entry gives more than one.

The roots: late 1960s

Before there were dance crews, the dance lived with the street gangs.

  1. Late 1960s Gangs burn to music

    Up Rock grew out of the street fights of the gangs. At first rockers imitated those fights with the gestures of the street; as the dance was practiced, they added the interpretation of the music and gestural jokes, mocking the other or playing with the instruments and the lyrics of the record, until it became a dance style of its own. In many cases, gang fights turned into Apache Lines: dance battles in place of violence. Veterans also recall that gangs in other places learned it through their Brooklyn chapters.

    When he was young there were gangs on the street, and the dance pulled him away from them. A passion, practiced with discipline, can open another path.

  2. 1967–1968 Born in Bushwick

    Up Rock was born in Bushwick, Brooklyn, in the late 1960s; most accounts give 1968. Two dancers created it, Rubberband Man and Apache, playing and having fun in the street: Rubberband, who also danced and won contests in other dances, and Apache, the joker. Rubberband Man is no longer with us.

    By one account it then crossed the Williamsburg Bridge to the streets of the Lower East Side of Manhattan.

  3. 1968–1970 A first generation

    A first generation of rockers was active around 1968–70, among them Little Dave, whose Little Dave Rockers grew into one of the big crews of the next decade. Brooklyn already had well-known DJs.

The 1970s: crews, contests and the jerk

In the 1970s the dance got its crews, its contests and its signature move, the jerk. Crews battled on Bushwick’s streets and in its church halls. Dates in this decade are the most disputed, so most entries give more than one.

  1. 1971–1973 Learning on the corner

    A rocker of the second generation, new to Bushwick in 1969, got hooked watching members of a local gang rock on a street corner.

  2. 1973 A contest in a church

    By one account the first rock contest was held in 1973 at St. Barbara’s Church in Bushwick, an account passed down from older rockers. Crazy Rob, a Bushwick DJ and promoter, ran rock contests in churches and clubs; for Ralph Casanova, “the rock grew because of him”.

  3. 1973–1974 Dynasty and Touch of Rock

    The Dynasty Rockers date themselves to 1973. Their website names Danny Boy and Carlos as founders, with Manny and Eddie Figueroa among the first members, and Ralph Casanova credits Carlos with the name, taken from a karate movie. Their great rival, Touch of Rock, took its name in October 1974 with Buz, Cuz and Duz; Buz wanted to give it “a little touch of rock”.

  4. 1973–1977 Names on the back

    Crews began wearing their name and the word “Rockers” on their shirts, although colors were a gang matter and could bring trouble. The Dynasty Rockers’ own accounts date their first lettering to 1973 or to 1976.

  5. 1974–1976 The jerk appears

    The jerk, a sharp drop toward the floor on an accent of the music, arrives in these years. The rockers who were there date it differently: one first saw it in October 1974, another saw no jerking in 1975 or 1976.

  6. 1976–1979 Crews multiply

    By one count there were more than a hundred crews in Bushwick alone by 1976–77, and some contests had Apache lines of more than 50 rockers.

    Late in the decade the name “uprock” took over. One account ties it to confusion with rock fans at jams. Another ties it to the Bushwick step spreading to other boroughs.

  7. Late 1970s The top rockers

    Ralph Casanova, who joined Dynasty in 1976, names the rockers he had to beat: Danny Boy and Carlos of Dynasty, Duz and Cuz of Touch of Rock, Rocky Nelson of MTC and Little Dave. His best battle, he says, was against Diana of Dynasty. Disco Eddie ranked his Disco Rockers with Dynasty and Touch of Rock as the top three crews.

  8. 1979 Into breaking

    One rocker first saw breaking in 1979 at a roller rink in Queens, and a breaking crew in Williamsburg dates its founding to the same year. How the two dances differ is explained on the Is Up Rock Top Rock? page.

The 1980s: beyond New York, and the fade

The early 1980s brought the first signs of the dance outside New York. By the middle of the decade breaking had taken over the contests, and rocking almost disappeared.

  1. 1980 King Uprock

    In 1980, at the Yellow Sunshine club on Bushwick Avenue, crews such as Touch of Rock, the Lil Dave Rockers, the Romantic Rockers, Rock with Class and Dynasty met in a rock contest. Ralph Casanova of Dynasty, then known as RC, beat Little Dave in the final and from then on called himself King Uprock.

  2. 1981 Contests and the Bronx

    Rock contests came in two kinds: one-on-one battles and routines with a partner. That year a Bronx club flyer advertised a rock dance contest, and one history dates the arrival of the uprock step in the Bronx to 1981.

  3. Early 1980s Beyond New York

    Dancers recall the dance in Hartford, Connecticut, where it was called outlawing, and in Florida, where by 1983 people simply called it Brooklyn.

  4. 1983–1985 The fade

    By one rocker’s account, after 1983 the contests were all breaking contests, and his generation came to an end around 1985. A 1984 breakdancing manual gave uprock a section of its own and noted that more and more dancers did it solo, without an opponent.

The 1990s: looking for the originals

In the 1990s Ralph “King Uprock” Casanova brought the dance back. Most of the original dancers had stopped decades before; he looked for them, brought them in as judges and began to teach again. Some rockers call it the rebirth.

  1. 1993–1994 The first Dollar Jams

    Dollar Jams, monthly jams with a one-dollar entry, began in 1993 or 1994, with free classes alongside them. By Ralph Casanova’s account Mr. Loose of Born To Rock started them with him, and Gary “Crumb” of Dynasty helped judge.

  2. 1995 A new generation

    In early 1995 Ralph Casanova drew Break Easy, a breaker of Breaking in Style who had learned to rock as a boy in Williamsburg, back to rocking. From then on Break Easy taught a new generation, and he later joined Dynasty.

  3. 1997–1999 Looking for the originals

    In 1997–98 Ralph Casanova went looking for the original Brooklyn rockers and took over the Dynasty Rockers as, in his words, “the new founder from the 90s”. Numbers, of the T.N.T. breaking crew, joined in 1998 as vice president and led the search with Burn One.

  4. 1999 Ridgewood

    In 1999 Ralph Casanova of the Dynasty Rockers set up a practice with the Greater Ridgewood Youth Council in the gym of I.S. 93 in Ridgewood, Queens. Up to 100 people aged 6 to 30 practiced there while original dancers taught them the foundation and the history of the form.

The 2000s: Folk Feet and trips abroad

In the 2000s Up Rock got a place in a Brooklyn folk dance program and reached Europe.

  1. 2002 Las Vegas, Florida, Switzerland

    By 2002 the Dynasty Rockers listed members in Las Vegas, Florida and Switzerland.

  2. 2003–2009 Folk Feet

    The Brooklyn Arts Council’s Folk Feet project documented and presented Brooklyn’s traditional dances from 2003 to 2009, uprocking among them.

  3. 2003 Thirty years of Dynasty

    On May 3, 2003, the Dynasty Rockers turned 30. Apache came in his Devil’s Rebels colors, Manny Figueroa received an award as an “Uprock Pioneer,” and Karyn “the Lish” of Breaking in Style won the uprock contest against Mr. Loose of Born To Rock.

  4. 2005–2007 France and Poland

    In 2005 Wary, a French rocker from Dijon, joined the Dynasty Rockers. Dancers in Poland saw the dance on video around 2006, and in 2007 Wary taught a two-day workshop there for about 50 dancers; that year Suseł and Wawix founded Rockin With Flavor, by one account Poland’s first rocking crew.

  5. 2009 Mastermind returns

    A new generation of the Mastermind Rockers, the Williamsburg crew, came together in 2009 around Mighty Mike, after original members gave him the name. Its rockers have since taken the dance to Poland, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea.

2010 to 2019: Russia, Poland and Asia

From about 2010 rockers from the United States visited Russia and Poland, and crews there and in Asia grew their own scenes.

  1. 2010–2012 Tours of Europe

    Cold Chris, a Dynasty Rocker from Las Vegas and a student of King Uprock, made three tours of Europe between 2010 and 2012, teaching in Zamość, Toruń and Żagań, and brought Foots of Dynasty on his 2012 Live To Rock tour. Poland also hosted Amigo Rock of Dynasty in 2010, Ringo of Mastermind in 2011 and Break Easy in 2012.

  2. 2011 Thirty-eight years in Phoenix

    The Dynasty Rockers held their 38th anniversary in Phoenix, Arizona, on October 29, 2011.

  3. 2013 Tver

    Russian TV covered a rock contest in Tver for the Dynasty Rockers’ 40th anniversary, held by For The Rock, which the presenter called the only rock crew in the country. Its member FT is, in Ralph Casanova’s words, his vice president in Russia.

  4. About 2018 A line of 150

    By one account an anniversary event in Russia gathered 150 rockers in an Apache line. In 2018 FT ran the rock dance contest at the Yalta Summer Jam.

  5. 2019 St. Petersburg

    In an interview recorded at a festival in St. Petersburg, one of the original rockers said the best rockers now live in Russia, with Poland, China and Korea also at a good level.

Today

For more than two decades Ralph “King Uprock” Casanova has been the one carrying the dance, with the Dynasty Rockers and the Brooklyn Arts Council, in New York and abroad. Today the dance has crews on several continents. Ralph names Dynasty chapters in Las Vegas, Boston, China, Korea, Spain and Colombia.

  1. 2020 For The Record

    Bushwick veterans met for “For The Record,” a round table on Vitamin Vision TV, so the first generation could tell its story. They disagreed openly on dates. That year one rocker said that New York, and even the United States, could not gather 20 solid rockers for a contest, while lines of 15 to 50 formed in Russia, Korea or Japan.

  2. 2021–2022 A magazine in Japan

    From November 1, 2021, to November 21, 2022, a Japanese group published “Weekly UPROCK MAG” on note.com: 58 articles on uprock, including translated interviews with original rockers. It also followed today’s crews, from Ultimate Style Rockers in South Korea to Jackey Burn of Mastermind in Taiwan.
