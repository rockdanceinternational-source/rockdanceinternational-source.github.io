Up Rock history: where the dance began

Up Rock was born on the streets of Bushwick, Brooklyn, in the late 1960s. The Dynasty Rockers' own accounts place its birth there in 1968. This timeline follows the main line of the story, from the first street corners to the rockers of Russia, Poland and Japan.

The roots: late 1960s

Before there were dance crews, the dance lived with the street gangs.

  1. Late 1960s Gangs burn to music

    Up Rock was inspired by the street fights between gangs. At first rockers imitated those fights with the gestures of the street; as the dance was practiced, they added the interpretation of the music and gestural jokes, mocking the other or playing with the instruments and the lyrics of the record, until it became a dance style of its own. In many cases, gang fights turned into Apache Lines: dance battles in place of violence. By Ralph Casanova’s account, gangs in other places learned it from their own chapters (local branches) in Brooklyn.

  2. 1967–1968 Born in Bushwick

    Up Rock was born in Bushwick, Brooklyn, in the late 1960s; the Dynasty Rockers’ own accounts give 1968. Two dancers created it, Rubberband Man and Apache, playing and having fun in the street: Rubberband, who also danced and won contests in other dances, and Apache, the joker. Rubberband Man is no longer with us.

    From Brooklyn the dance crossed the Williamsburg Bridge, which joins Brooklyn and Manhattan, and reached the streets and parks of the Lower East Side, as Fred Rolle, a rocker from that neighborhood, writes.

  3. 1968–1970 A first generation

    By Ralph Casanova’s account, a first generation of rockers was active from 1968–69 or 1969–70, among them Little Dave, whose Little Dave Rockers grew into one of the big crews of the next decade. The same account says Brooklyn already had well-known DJs, such as Grandmaster Flowers.

The 1970s: crews, contests and the jerk

In the 1970s the dance got its crews, its contests and its signature move, the jerk. Crews battled on Bushwick’s streets and in its church halls. Dates in this decade are the most disputed, so most entries give more than one.

  2. 1971–1973 Learning on the corner

    Members of local gangs such as the Devil’s Rebels rocked on Bushwick street corners, and younger kids picked the dance up there.

    When he was young there were gangs on the street, and the dance pulled him away from them. A passion, practiced with discipline, can open another path.

  3. 1973 A contest in a church

    Older rockers told Ralph Casanova of the first rock contest, in 1973 at St. Barbara’s Church in Bushwick. Crazy Rob, a Bushwick promoter, took rock contests from the streets into church halls and rec centers, as the Dynasty Rockers’ website tells it. Buz of Touch of Rock remembers his contests too, and in Ralph’s words, “the rock grew because of him”.

  4. 1973–1974 Dynasty and Touch of Rock

    The Dynasty Rockers (@dynastyrockers) date themselves to 1973. Their website names Danny Boy and Carlos as founders, with Manny and Eddie Figueroa among the first members, and by Ralph Casanova’s account Carlos took the name from a karate movie. Their great rival was Touch of Rock, with Buz, Cuz and Duz. It grew out of a group that Buz calls the Nightbreakers, and Duz dates the new name to October 1974; in Buz’s words, “We wanted to give it a little touch of rock”.

  5. 1973–1977 Names on the back

    Crews began wearing their name and the word “Rockers” on their shirts, although colors were a gang matter and could bring trouble. Depending on the account, Dynasty’s first lettering dates from 1973 or from 1976.

  6. 1974–1976 The jerk appears

    The jerk is a sudden movement of the body, usually down toward the floor, on an accent of the music, counted “1, 2, 3 and 4”. The rockers who were there do not agree on when it appeared. Duz says he saw it, and began learning it, in October 1974, while Noel, of the same crew, saw no jerking in 1975 or 1976, and Charlie Uprock wrote that before 1976 there were no jerks.

  7. 1976–1979 Crews multiply

    By Ralph Casanova’s account, crews boomed in 1976–77, with more than a hundred in Bushwick alone. The Mastermind Rockers’ website says Frankie Viejo and Fano founded the crew in 1977. The Dynasty Rockers’ website recalls Apache lines of more than 50 rockers at some contests, and another veteran remembers lines of up to 40 people a side.

    Over time the name “uprock” appeared beside “rock”, the name many veterans still use. Break Easy has dated the change both to the late 1970s and to the early 1970s, and tied it to confusion with rock fans at jams; the book Underground Dance Masters (2012) ties it to the Bushwick step spreading to other boroughs.

  8. Late 1970s The top rockers

    By Ralph Casanova’s account, the rockers to beat were Danny Boy and Carlos of Dynasty, Duz and Cuz of Touch of Rock, Rocky Nelson of MTC and Little Dave. Diana led Dynasty’s girls, and Ralph calls his battle against her the best he ever had. Disco Eddie ranked his Disco Rockers third among the top three crews, after Dynasty and Touch of Rock.

  9. 1979 Into breaking

    By Ralph Casanova’s account, breaking was danced at a roller rink in Queens in 1979, and Breaking in Style, a Williamsburg breaking crew, gives 1979 as its founding year, although members’ profiles on the same site say 1980. How the two dances differ is explained on the Is Up Rock Top Rock? page.

The 1980s: beyond New York, and the fade

By the early 1980s there were signs of the dance outside New York. By the middle of the decade breaking had taken over the contests, and rocking almost disappeared.

  1. 1980s Brooklyn and Bronx crews

    Five crews on the Dynasty Rockers’ list belong to the 1980s; here they are in the order of the list:

    • Brooklyn Vicious Dancers (Coney Island, 1980)
    • Grant Crew (Brooklyn)
    • Home Boys Only (the Bronx, 1980)
    • Home Girls Only (the Bronx)
    • Furious Rockers (Coney Island, 1980 or 1981)

    The years come from the founders themselves and, for Home Boys Only, from an interview with one of its members, Dollar Bill. For Home Girls Only, which comes up in that interview, and for Grant Crew, the decade is our reading and the year they started is not recorded.

  2. 1980 King Uprock

    As Ralph Casanova and the Dynasty Rockers’ website tell it, in 1980 crews such as Touch of Rock, the Lil Dave Rockers, the Romantic Rockers, Rock with Class and Dynasty met in a rock contest at the Yellow Sunshine club on Bushwick Avenue, and Ralph, then known as RC, beat Little Dave in the final. From then on he called himself King Uprock.

  3. 1981 Contests and the Bronx

    Rock contests came in two kinds: one-on-one battles and routines with a partner. That year a flyer for a club in the Bronx advertised a rock dance contest. The book Underground Dance Masters (2012) says the uprock step started being done in the Bronx in 1981.

  4. Early 1980s Beyond New York

    By the account of a dancer who grew up in Hartford, Connecticut, a gang there, the Savage Nomads from New York, did the dance and called it outlawing, and there was talk of rock dance contests in Holyoke, Massachusetts, around 1982–84. In Florida in 1983, by the same account, people simply called it Brooklyn, a name Joseph Schloss also records.

    Outlaws: the rockers’ word for gang members.

  5. 1983–1985 The fade

    By Ralph Casanova’s account, after 1983–84 the contests were all breaking contests, and the generation of rockers of the 1970s came to an end around 1985; another veteran says 1983 was probably the last time he rocked. A 1984 breakdancing manual gave uprock a section of its own and noted that more and more dancers did it solo, without an opponent.

The 1990s: looking for the originals

In the 1990s Ralph “King Uprock” Casanova brought the dance back, at the head of a small group that, by Break Easy’s account, included Mr. Loose, Burn One and Charlie Uprock. Most of the original dancers had stopped about a decade before; he looked for them, brought them in as judges and began to teach again. Some rockers call it the rebirth.

  1. 1993–1994 The first Dollar Jams

    Dollar Jams, monthly jams with a one-dollar entry started by Ralph Casanova, began in 1993 or 1994 by his dates, with free classes alongside them. There were trophies for breaking, uprock and popping. By his account Mr. Loose of Born To Rock started them with him, and Gary “Crumb” of Dynasty helped judge. In 1994 Charlie Uprock also held a rock competition with DJ Kool, Break Easy recalls.

  2. 1995 A new generation

    In early 1995 Ralph Casanova drew Break Easy, a breaker of Breaking in Style who had learned to rock as a boy in Williamsburg, back to rocking. From then on Break Easy taught a new generation of b-boys, and later rocking too; he joined Dynasty in 2002. In December 1995 Charlie Uprock organized a jam, by Wary The Warrior’s account.

  3. 1997–1999 Looking for the originals

    In 1997–98 Ralph Casanova went looking for the original Brooklyn rockers. With the blessing of Danny Boy, one of the crew’s founders, he took over the Dynasty Rockers as, in his words, “the new founder from the 90s”. The Dynasty Rockers’ website of 2002 says two b-boys, Numbers of the T.N.T. breaking crew and Burn One, had started a search for the original rockers in the mid-1990s. Numbers joined Dynasty in 1998 and later became its vice president; by Break Easy’s account, he flew King Uprock to Miami.

  4. 1999 Ridgewood

    In 1999 Ralph Casanova of the Dynasty Rockers set up a practice session with the Greater Ridgewood Youth Council in the gym of I.S. 93 in Ridgewood, Queens, where he coached uprock. By 2002 the Village Voice called it one of the largest practice spots on the East Coast. Up to 100 people aged 6 to 30 came, and original rockers taught them the basics, which dancers call the foundation, and the history of the form.

  5. 1999 Colombian Rockers

    By the crew’s own account, Colombian Rockers (@colom_bianrockers) began to take shape in Bogotá in 1999.

The 2000s: Folk Feet and trips abroad

In the 2000s Up Rock got a place in a Brooklyn folk dance program and reached Europe.

  1. 2002 Las Vegas, Florida, Switzerland

    By 2002 the Dynasty Rockers listed members in Las Vegas, Florida and Switzerland.

  2. 2003–2009 Folk Feet

    The Brooklyn Arts Council’s Folk Feet project documented and presented Brooklyn’s traditional dances from 2003 to 2009, uprocking among them.

  3. 2003 Thirty years of Dynasty

    On May 3, 2003, the Dynasty Rockers turned 30. Apache came in his Devil’s Rebels colors, Manny Figueroa, whom the crew’s site calls an “Uprock Pioneer,” received an award for his achievements in uprock history, and Karyn “the Lish” of Breaking in Style won the uprock contest against Mr. Loose of Born To Rock.

  4. 2005–2007 France and Poland

    In 2005 Wary, a French rocker from Dijon, joined the Dynasty Rockers. By his account, Crazy Smooth joined that year too, and in April Paulskeee came to Dijon and shared the little he had learned with King Uprock in New York. The Dynasty Rockers took uprock to Total Session 7 in France, held in September 2005 by Wary’s dates. According to a 2019 Polish article, a video from that event drew dancers in Poland to the dance around 2006. In 2007, the article says, Wary taught a two-day workshop in Ostrów Wielkopolski for about 50 dancers, and that year Suseł and Wawix founded the rocking crew Rockin With Flavor.

  5. 2005 VII Gems Rock Division

    VII Gems Rock Division was formed in 2005, by Wary’s account, with Ken Swift, Burn One, Spice, Nako, Lil’Star, Mr. Loose, Clarkie, Eve and Paydro among its members.

  6. 2006 Forever We Rock

    By Rock Bandit’s account, he founded Forever We Rock in San Francisco in 2006 with PJAY71 and Rob Nasty.

  7. 2006–2009 KLP and Charlie Uprock

    Around 2006–2007 the French crew KLP studied with Charlie Uprock and Mike and danced with Rock With Style, by Wary’s account. KLP later invited Charlie Uprock to its anniversary, and in 2009 he gave a workshop in Nantes.

  8. 2007–2013 Workshops in Europe and São Paulo

    By his account, Wary taught Rock Dance workshops in Geneva in May 2007 (organized by Menad), in Poland in June 2007, in Poznań in 2009 and in Warsaw in 2013. In 2008 he and Crazy Smooth taught one at Circle Kingz in Lausanne for about 80 people; in 2009 he taught at Street Science in Rotterdam and, in December, at the Master Crews event in São Paulo, where 150 dancers signed up for 50 places. Since then, he says, he has taught more than 200 Rock Dance workshops around the world.

  9. 2008 Time 2 Rock

    In 2008 Rock Bandit and Suer founded Time 2 Rock (@time2rockdance) in San Diego, by Rock Bandit’s account.

  10. 2009 Mastermind returns

    In 2009 Mighty Mike and Ringo, with the blessing of original members, started a new generation of the Mastermind Rockers, the Williamsburg crew. The crew says it has since taken the dance to Japan, Taiwan, South Korea and Poland. Frankie Viejo and Fano had founded the crew in 1977, according to its website, mastermindrockers.com; on Instagram it is @mastermindrockers.

  11. 2009 For The Rock

    For The Rock (@apache_line) was founded in 2009 in Tver, Russia, by the crew’s own account.

  12. 2009 Indígenas Rockers

    By its founder’s account, Indígenas Rockers was created in Bogotá in 2009 by Bboy Pilot (@bboy_pilot_rock). Pilot came from Street Course, his group since 1994, where he had been searching for the form of Up Rock within Breaking. In 2010 and 2011 he took his style, which he describes as “purely Latin”, to the streets of the Bronx and Brooklyn. The crew works in Breaking and Rock Dance.

2010 to 2019: Russia, Poland and Asia

From about 2010 Russian dancers, FT among them, invited the Dynasty Rockers to Russia, and by the crew’s account the visits went on almost every year until the pandemic. Rockers from Dynasty and Mastermind visited Poland, and crews there and in Asia grew their own scenes.

  1. 2010–2012 Tours of Europe

    Cold Chris, a Las Vegas-based Dynasty Rocker, made three tours of Europe between 2010 and 2012, teaching in Zamość, Toruń and Żagań, and brought Foots of Dynasty on his 2012 Live To Rock tour. According to the 2019 Polish article, Poland also hosted Amigo of Dynasty in 2010, Ringo of Mastermind in 2011 and Break Easy in 2012.

  2. 2010 Just4Rockers, Paris

    In April 2010, at Just4Rockers in Paris, Yass (RTR/MZK) and Wary danced an exhibition battle to make Rock Dance known in France and help the local scene learn it, by Wary’s account.

  3. 2011 Thirty-eight years in Phoenix

    The Dynasty Rockers held their 38th anniversary in Phoenix, Arizona, on October 29, 2011.

  4. 2011 Kossassins

    Kossassins was founded in 2011 by Wary The Warrior (@warythewarrior), the rocker from Dijon who had joined the Dynasty Rockers in 2005, and Crazy Smooth, between Ottawa, Canada, and Dijon, France, by Wary’s account.

  5. 2013 Tver

    Russian TV covered a rock contest in Tver for the Dynasty Rockers’ 40th anniversary, held by For The Rock. Its member FT is, by Dynasty’s own account, the Dynasty Rockers’ vice president in Russia.

  6. 2013 Taipei

    By the account of The Joint (@thejointjam), the Monday Rocking Session has met in Taipei every week since 2013, free and open to all; it began when Jacky Burnz of the Mastermind Rockers came back from New York and began training rockers in Taiwan. That same year Jacky Burnz and Yell G, both of the Mastermind Rockers, founded Rock Fantastic (@rockfantastic2013) with six other rockers; rooted in Taipei, it is now a crew of 15 members from all over Taiwan, by its own account. Since 2024 Scottie has held Rock Free Bar, a Rocking party, twice a month.

  7. 2015 Rock’n’Raw

    According to a 2019 Polish article, Kostek, Andy, Bożna, Ewa and Patrik founded the crew Rock’n’Raw (@rocknrawrockers) in 2015, and its 4th anniversary was set for December 7, 2019, in Warsaw. At its 10th Anniversary Jam, the Dynasty Rockers battled BVD.

  8. 2018–2019 A line of 150

    In 2018 FT was at the Yalta Summer Jam, where Grinch won the rock dance contest. In 2019 Russian TV covered For The Rock’s tenth anniversary. A tenth-anniversary event in Russia, recalled in a 2022 interview as “like four years ago”, put 150 rockers in an Apache line.

  9. 2019 St. Petersburg

    In an interview recorded at a festival in St. Petersburg, Ralph Casanova spoke of a new generation of dancers and of the level of the rockers in Russia, Poland, China and Korea.

  10. 2019 Cold As1an Rockers (C1R)

    By Babylon’s account and by its own profile, Cold As1an Rockers (@coldas1anrockers), known as C1R, was founded in 2019. The profile carries the flags of Japan, China, Taiwan and Korea.

Today

For more than two decades Ralph “King Uprock” Casanova has been the one carrying the dance, with the Dynasty Rockers and the Brooklyn Arts Council, in New York and abroad. Today the dance has crews on several continents. Ralph says Dynasty has members in Russia, Las Vegas, Boston, China, Korea, Spain (Moysex, a Dynasty Rocker since 2006) and Colombia.

  1. 2020 For The Record

    Brooklyn veterans met for “For The Record,” a round table published by Vitamin Vision TV, so the early generations could tell their story. They disagreed openly on dates. That year, in another Vitamin Vision TV interview, the interviewer asked why New York, and even the United States, could not gather 20 solid rockers for a contest, while lines of 15 to 50 formed in Russia, Korea or Japan. The answer: “It’s the negativity, it’s the lies, the disrespect.” In Russia, by contrast, “They believe in show and prove”.

  2. 2021–2022 A magazine in Japan

    From November 1, 2021, to November 21, 2022, a Japanese group published “Weekly UPROCK MAG” on note.com: 58 articles, almost all on uprock, among them interviews with the OG Frank Rojas and a translated interview with Ringo. It also followed today’s crews, from Ultimate Style Rockers in South Korea to Jackey Burn of Mastermind in Taiwan.

  3. 2022 Dark Circles, Pereira

    By the account of Wai One, of BVD Rocking Crew, Dark Circles (@darkcircles.22) is a hip hop jam held in Pereira, Colombia, since 2022, with Rocking at its center alongside Graffiti, Breaking, MCs and DJs. The first edition was on April 30, 2022, with a visit from Papo Luv, founder of BVD (@theoriginalpapoluv). Guest rockers have come from Chile, Bolivia, Mexico, the United States, Venezuela and Colombia.

  4. 2022 Rockdance Hong Kong

    In August 2022 Justroc, of MZK and Floor Gangz, founded the Rockdance Hong Kong community (@rockdance852) with the support of Tiga, of Revolution, and Match, by the community’s own account.

  5. 2023 Brooklyn Soul Rockers and Rock Wars, Bogotá

    By the account of DnyRoc (@dnyroc), Brooklyn Soul Rockers was born in Bogotá in 2023 to join the crews already active in Colombia, with the first visit of Ralph “King Uprock” Casanova and the aim of keeping the original Bushwick style alive through new students. That first visit was organized and backed by Denny Zamudio Correa, DnyRoc. DnyRoc’s video calls that event Rock Wars 2023; Rock Wars Session Vol. 1 followed in 2024 and Rock Wars Session II in 2026, in Bogotá.

  6. 2024 La Meseta Rockers

    By its own account, La Meseta Rockers (@la_meseta_rockers) has been a rocking crew since 2024, based in Spain.

  7. 2024 Nasty With Burns, Pereira

    Also by Wai One’s account, the weekly rocking sessions of the Pereira Rock Session began in Pereira in 2024. Out of them came Nasty With Burns (@nastywithburns), a crew of rockers from the city, mentored and led by Wai One (@waiuno1), an active member of BVD Rocking Crew.

  8. 2025 Don’t Stop The Rock!

    In January 2025 Wary The Warrior published Don’t Stop The Rock!, a book devoted entirely to Rocking, with illustrations by Dan Lish.

  9. 2025 Funk Fusion Jam, Athens

    Funk Fusion Jam (@funkfusionjam), organized by The Rock Brothers, Aris and Denis, held its first edition in Athens on February 2, 2025, and its second on February 1, 2026, by the event’s account. FT, Bozna and Exect judged the Rock Dance.

  10. 2025 Rockin Park Sakashita, Tokyo

    In November 2025 Kouta asked Haji (@haji.me.n) to teach him Rocking, and the two began practicing together in Itabashi, Tokyo, by Haji’s account. Their group, Rockin Park Sakashita, is on Instagram as @rockin.park.sakashita.

  11. November 2026 Solntsevo Rock, Moscow

    Coming up: on November 28, 2026, Solntsevo Rock holds its first 1x1 Rock Dance battle in western Moscow, Russia, with an apache line, the battle and a party. The judges are Bochrock, Laika and Lev, who also play the music with Lil Igor. @bochrock announced it in the official flyer on Instagram.

  12. December 2026 Rock Fantastic 13th Anniversary

    Coming up: from December 11 to 13, 2026, Rock Fantastic Crew celebrates its 13th anniversary in Taipei, Taiwan, with Rocking 1vs1 battles in three categories: U12, U16 and Open. The guests and judges are Peaky Rock, from France, and Gonzo and KNUKL, from South Korea. The organizers are on Instagram at @thejointjam and @rockfantastic2013. The official flyer is on Instagram.

  13. February 2027 Funk Fusion Jam, Athens

    Coming up: the next edition of Funk Fusion Jam is on February 21, 2027, in Athens, the last day of a three-day jam, the event says. It is on Instagram at @funkfusionjam.

  14. October 2027 Rockdance Hong Kong 5th anniversary jam

    Coming up: in October 2027, Rockdance Hong Kong celebrates its 5th anniversary with a jam in Hong Kong. The exact day is still to be announced. The community is on Instagram at @rockdance852.

  15. 2027 RDI - Rock Dance International

    On October 26, 2026, the non-profit association RDI - Rock Dance International is born, led by Moysex and devoted to one dance: Up Rock, also called Rock Dance. Its aim is to preserve the dance as intangible cultural heritage (its history, its technique, its music and the memory of the people who created it and passed it on), to tell its story with rigor and with respect for Up Rock / Rock Dance as the forerunner of Breaking and for all its artists (dancers, DJs, musicians, singers, teachers, students, judges, competitors and organizers), to take it to new countries and audiences, to bring its community together across the world and across generations, and to help teachers, judges and organizers make a decent living from it. This timeline is part of that work.
