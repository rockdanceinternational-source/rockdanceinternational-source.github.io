Colombian Rockers

Colombian Rockers is a crew from Bogotá, Colombia, that dances Breaking, Rock Dance and Up Rock. By its own account it began to take shape in 1999, under the direction of its founder, Kurtys Flave.

On this page

  2. The music first
  3. La Tandra
  4. Alliances
  5. The logo

This page draws on the memoir Colombian Rockers sent us in November 2026, which the crew presents as its own testimony, not as an independently checked chronology. The crew is on Instagram at @colom_bianrockers. For its place in the wider story, see the history timeline.

How the crew began

Kurtys Flave learned to dance as a boy at parties and minitecas, Bogotá’s party sound systems, where the DJs played disco, funk, house, hard house and breakbeats. In 1995 that scene drew him to Breaking. Information was hard to find in those years: it came through dancers who traveled and recordings passed from hand to hand, often in fragments. From 1999 he began to build the name, the logo and the identity of Colombian Rockers, from the Las Cruces neighborhood in central Bogotá. On Instagram the crew dates its work to 1995, under the direction of Kurtys Flave. The crew’s printed history names him as its director and Panda and Pinky among its members.

The music first

For Colombian Rockers the music is a guide, not a backing track. The crew studies each record, with its breaks, cuts, changes and instrumental passages, and listens closely to the DJ. In its words, Freestyle means enjoying the music and answering it, but never dancing without codes. Kurtys Flave recalls that in late-1990s Bogotá, Breaking was judged mostly by power moves such as the headspin, and that only three or four dancers he knew put style and musicality first.

La Tandra

The memoir also records La Tandra, a dance developed in Colombia. Some of its moves can recall Up Rock, but it has a logic of its own: the feet mark the beat very close to the floor, picking out percussion such as timbales and bongos, and by the crew’s account it grew out of the House and Hard House scenes of Bogotá. The crew asks that it be seen in its own context, not as a copy or a variant of Up Rock.

Alliances

Colombian Rockers works with three allied groups. Territorio Apache describes itself as a collective devoted to Up Rock and, according to the memoir, is linked to the Bronx. Hipstep Zuluz, also known as Hipstep Massive Worldwide, is a Hip Hop dance collective that runs training and community work in Bogotá. Fahrenheit 75 Division (FH75) comes from the Bronx, New York, and Kurtys Flave wears its insignia in Colombia. By his account he represents these groups locally in Bogotá, without taking the place of their founders or of their own history.

The logo

The crew designed its own logo. The circle stands for the cypher; the pre-Columbian figure, for the first peoples of Colombia; the lightning bolts, for the energy of the rockers and of the music; the lines and ridges, for Colombia’s mountains and for the cuts and changes in a record. Red stands for passion and resistance, and black and white for light and shadow.
