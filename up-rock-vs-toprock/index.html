Is Up Rock Top Rock?

Top Rock is what b-boys and b-girls dance standing, before going down to the floor. Up Rock is a battle dance of its own, and it came first: two rockers face off for a whole record, improvising on the music. Here are the differences.

The differences

Up Rock Top Rock (in breaking)
What it is A battle dance: two rockers face off and improvise on the music What b-boys and b-girls dance standing, before going down to the floor
The moves Freestyle, jerks and burns The intro of a set, before any b-boy or b-girl combination of Footwork, Freezes and Power Moves
How long The whole song Breaking is danced to the break of the song (usually 30 to 60 seconds)
The battle Face to face, often in an Apache Line In a circle
The DJ Records blended and mixed Cuts and scratches

Where the names cross

Both words grow out of rock. Many early dancers say Up Rock was first called rock or rocking, and rocking was used in the Bronx too. Then the names crossed. In the Brooklyn account, as Up Rock faded from the mid-1980s it survived only in a modified form called Top Rock, danced by breakers. In the Bronx account, the breakers of 1979 to 1984 had their own simplified take on rocking, called Top Rocking or uprocking. When and why the dance itself became Up Rock is on What is Up Rock?.

Is Top Rock a form of Up Rock?

Top Rock comes from rocking. Rocking came first and its steps are more complex; b-boys and b-girls dance them in a simpler form, because the complexity of breaking is on the floor.

  • A simpler version. Top Rock is the modified Up Rock danced in breaking, an imitation of rocking. Rock and breaking are two different dances, like merengue and salsa. A crew’s 30th anniversary flyer in 2003 listed Uprock and B-boy as separate contests.
  • In breaking. The breakers’ own simplified take on rocking was called Top Rocking or uprocking. Some breaking books and websites list uprock as one more element of breaking; for rockers, rocking came first.

Uprock vs downrock

Downrock, also written down rock, downrocking or floor rock, names rocking done on or near the floor. What exactly it covers is disputed.

In the Brooklyn accounts, downrocking was the local word for the floor dancing the Bronx called breaking, and the Bronx extended it with spins, glides and other moves. The Bronx account describes the footwork of the Bronx crews of 1975 to 1979 as steps done on all fours, later called downrocking.

Can a breaker learn Up Rock?

Yes, and dancers have crossed over in both directions. Expect it to take time: in a veteran’s view, breaking can be learned faster. The moves are on the fundamentals page.

So, is Up Rock Top Rock?

No. Top Rock is the intro of a breaking set: a few steps standing before the Footwork, Freezes and Power Moves. Up Rock is a whole dance, with its own battle, its own music and its own rules, danced from the first bar of the record to the last. Top Rock came out of rocking, not the other way around.
